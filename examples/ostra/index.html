Why four

Nothing here is rare. It is just particular: a fig cured in Castro Marim salt, cork bark the week it comes off the tree, a weed that grows out of a cliff, a stone ten minutes after rain.

The four. 50 ml each, €165 each, one material each.

Figo

Salt-cured fig

Castro Marim, Algarve

Black figs split and packed in flor de sal from the Castro Marim pans. The salt draws out the jam and leaves the leather. Milky for an hour, then dry, then almost savoury.

Sobreiro

Cork oak

Comporta, Alentejo coast

Cork comes off the tree in July, once every nine years, and leaves the trunk the colour of brick. This is that week: dry bark, warm dust, and resin drifting over from the pines in the next field.

Funcho

Sea fennel

Cabo Espichel

Crithmum maritimum grows out of the limestone at Cabo Espichel, where little else will. Crush a stem and you get celery, lemon peel and iodine. We kept it sharp and let it soften only at the very end.
